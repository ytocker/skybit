# Critique — Barramundi-Spirit (x-ray fish-beast) — ROUND 1

VERDICT: ITERATE

Strong, original lead. The CHARACTER read is the best of the Mokoi brood at hero
scale and the lane pin is clean — but the round confirms the GD's own flag: at
true 32px the see-through SKELETON (the whole concept) loses to the eye and the
rust body. Bones must dominate the compact bake before this ships. One focused
round fixes it.

---

## Verdict rationale

The hook is "see right through a chubby fish to its glowing bones." At hero scale
that lands beautifully. But the gameplay-chip column (c) — the only view that
matters for a boss seen in motion against a scrolling sky — shows the spine + ribs
reading as a faint thread, dimmer than the body and out-shouted by the big amber
eye. The tell that defines this character is the FIRST thing to dissolve at size.
That is a ship-blocker for a concept whose entire identity is the x-ray read, so
ITERATE, not SHIP-READY. Not a RE-ROLL: silhouette, lane, pillar, and charm are
all right; this is a value/weight fix on the one element that carries the idea.

---

## Strongest / weakest

- **Strongest:** the hero silhouette + lane. A fat, wide-eyed almond fish with
  ochre fin-tips is instantly a CHARACTER and unmistakably the "side-on x-ray
  animal" KIND — nothing else in the roster of 15 shares it. Hematite-rust body
  with ochre fin-tips sits cleanly in the red-earth lane, distinct from source
  Mokoi (charcoal-dominant) and from Quinkan (charcoal+red). Scary-CUTE is dialed
  right: adorable until you notice the bones.
- **Weakest:** the x-ray tell at 32px. The skeleton is too thin, too low-contrast
  against the rust, and the eye is too dominant — so the compact chip reads as
  "rust fish with a big eye," not "see-through fish." The concept's payload is the
  part that fails the size test.

---

## Per-aspect KEEP / FIX

### 1. FLAT-GRAPHIC fidelity — KEEP
Saturated flat fills, hard charcoal keyline + 1px outline, detail via pattern not
3D triad — all correct and on-house-style. No gradient creep, no faux-shading. The
fish-medallion cap and pillar bands are flat-graphic clean. Hold this exactly.

### 2. The see-through tell at 32px — FIX (ship-blocker, ruling below)
This is the round's whole verdict. See "Ruling" + directives 1–3.

### 3. Re-spec compliance (spine/ribs/organ) — MOSTLY KEEP, tighten
The hero already obeys the sparse re-spec: a bold charcoal spine, ~4 ribs per
side, one pipeclay organ-lozenge — no dense rib-comb, good. The failure is not
rib COUNT, it's rib WEIGHT and CONTRAST in the bake. Keep the sparse count; make
the few lines that remain heavier and brighter so they survive downscale.

### 4. Cross-set PIN — KEEP
Hematite-rust dominant, yellow-ochre fin-tips only, ember confined to the cap.
Reads as the red-earth lane and separates cleanly from Mokoi and the rest of the
brood. The grayscale tell panel confirms hue-independent separation. No change.

### 5. Pillar — KEEP with one note
The weir-pole / fish-trap shaft (rib-ladder band + dot-row band per repeat) echoes
the body lattice and mirrors clean, bottom-rooted, ember cap-confined — exactly to
brief. One concern carried into directives: the pillar's rib-ladder is currently
brighter/heavier than the HERO's spine, so the prop out-reads the creature it
derives from. The body must be at least as bold as its own pillar.

---

## RULING — must the see-through tell dominate more? YES.

The x-ray read is the concept. It must be the SECOND thing you see after the
silhouette, and it must beat the eye for attention. Concrete fix, in priority:

- **Fatten + brighten the spine.** In the 32px bake the spine must be a single
  unbroken pipeclay-white (or near-white) bar ~2px at gameplay scale, not a thin
  charcoal hairline. Render it at hero scale as the thickest internal line, then
  let downscale thin it to a clean 2px. It should be the brightest internal mass.
- **Make ribs read as 3, not 4, in the bake.** 4 ribs/side is fine at hero scale
  but combs to mush at 32px. Drop the compact-bake rib read to 3 bold pipeclay
  ribs per side, each clearly separated, each as bright as the spine.
- **Demote the eye.** The amber eye currently wins the value fight. Shrink it
  ~20–25%, and either ring it in charcoal or drop its inner-white sheen so it stops
  competing with the bones for the brightest-mass slot. The bones, not the eye,
  must be the focal after the silhouette.
- **Darken the body behind the bones.** Push the rust body ~10–15% darker in the
  belly zone the skeleton sits over (still flat, just a darker flat region or a
  charcoal under-plate), so the pipeclay lattice has the contrast to pop. Right now
  bones-on-rust is too close in value — that's why the tell goes faint.

The test: in the neutral 32px chip, a player should read "I can see its bones"
before they read "it has a big eye." Round 1 fails that order; Round 2 must pass it.

---

## Iteration directives (prioritized punch list)

1. **Spine = brightest internal mass.** Re-render as a single thick pipeclay-white
   bar that bakes to ~2px at 32px, unbroken nose-to-tail. It must out-value the eye.
2. **3 bold ribs per side in the bake** (keep 4 at hero if you like, but ensure the
   downscale resolves to 3 cleanly separated bright ribs — no comb).
3. **Darken the body under the skeleton ~10–15%** (flat darker rust or a charcoal
   under-plate) to give the bones contrast; keep the outer body the lane-rust hue.
4. **Shrink the eye ~20–25% and kill its white sheen / ring it in charcoal** so it
   stops out-shouting the bones.
5. **Add one organ-lozenge brightness pass** — the single pipeclay organ should be
   the warm-glow focal of the x-ray, ember-tinted at its core (within cap-confined
   discipline this stays subtle — a faint ember-warm pipeclay, not a glow ring).
6. **Body must out-bold its own pillar.** The hero spine/ribs should be at least as
   bright/thick as the pillar's rib-ladder band; right now the prop reads bolder
   than the creature.
7. **Re-bake all three 32px chips (day/night/neutral) and re-audit grayscale** —
   the bones must survive the grayscale tell as the dominant internal pattern, not
   the eye. Surface the new chip column so the order-of-read can be re-judged.
