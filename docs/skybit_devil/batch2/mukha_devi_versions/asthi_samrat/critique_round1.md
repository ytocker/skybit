VERDICT: ITERATE

# ASTHI-SAMRAT — Round 1 critique

A strong, confident first round with a genuinely distinct architecture. Clears the austerity bar and the
Stupika-distinctness gate cleanly, but ONE structural failure undercuts the thesis: the hero does NOT read
as twelve-armed, and the stepped-cornice tell is too soft to survive at 32px. Both fixable without re-roll.

## Ranking (strongest → weakest)
1. **Accent / PURE-bone austerity — EXCELLENT, lock it.** Bone-only; sole non-bone note = ink keyline +
   cold steel-grey socket-pin w/ faint cold-white glow on the gap-cap. No stray hue anywhere (hero, both
   chips, pillar). Demonstrably the "pure pole"; reads completely differently from Stupika gilt + Mukha rose.
2. **Distinctness from Stupika — PASS (key risk beaten).** Stupika = smooth tapering cone-stupa, curved
   domes, single face, gilt swag, ZERO arms. Asthi-Samrat = hard rectilinear stacked block w/ side-poles.
3. **Proportion / monumentality — GOOD.** Bottom-rooted, widest at base, blocky, grounded. Heaviest base.
4. **Pillar — GOOD.** Clean mirror, rooted both ends, cold socket-glow caps the gap, square grooves tile.
5. **Stepped-cornice silhouette tell — PARTIAL.** Three tiers present but steps shallow — width drop small,
   overhangs barely break the vertical edge; blackout leans "chess-rook/single block." On the 32px night
   chip the tier separations nearly vanish into a featureless post.
6. **Multi-arm read — WEAKEST (the real failure).** Per brief this is TWELVE arms. They read as skull
   GARLANDS / a colonnade of little posts, not ARMS gripping standards — no shoulder→elbow→hand line,
   no arm-gesture. Against Mukha's unmistakable six radiating limbs, it currently reads as armless
   architecture — dangerously close to the ONE thing it must not be (Stupika is the armless sibling).

## Round-2 punch list (prioritized)
1. **Make the arms read as ARMS (top priority).** Give each of the 12 standards a visible bone-arm bracket
   — short upper-arm stub off the central monolith, a hard right-angle elbow, forearm rising into the
   banner-pole it grips. Even a 2-segment L-bracket per arm flips the read from "colonnade" to "twelve
   arms holding skull-standards." The rigid right-angle reinforces the architectural thesis — lean in.
   The hands gripping the poles are where the arm-end-ornament DNA lives.
2. **Deepen the cornice steps.** Width drop base 100% → mid 78% → top 58%, each cornice a hard OVERHANGING
   lip (1–2px dark groove UNDER each cornice + a small horizontal overhang that breaks the silhouette
   edge). Blackout should show three obvious notched steps, not a smooth rook.
3. **Carry cornice lines to 32px NIGHT.** Make the tier grooves the single darkest ink value + a 1px
   cold-white sheen on each cornice top-face, so each separation survives as light-over-dark on the night
   biome. Confirm you can count 3 tiers in a re-rendered night chip.
4. **Verify the 12-count is legible, or commit to a clear sub-count.** After adding brackets, if 4-per-tier
   crowds at 32px it's fine for the chip to read "many arms," but the HERO must let a viewer count 4
   across at least the base tier. Keep clear negative gaps so poles don't merge into a fence.
5. **Tiny-skull finials survive shrink.** Each finial ≥2px light blob over a dark socket on the hero; on
   the 32px chip they can simplify to a bright dotted top rail (still reads as a crown of points).
6. **Optional pillar polish.** One extra value-step on the cornice top-face so pillar + hero share the
   cold sheen-edge language.

Nail directives 1 and 2 and this is a ship-ready, genuinely distinct monumental pole.
