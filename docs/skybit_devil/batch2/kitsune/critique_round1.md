# Kitsune — round 1 critique (art-director)

A genuinely strong first pass. The face is the best-realized head in the
batch-2 Japanese section so far — the sly squint, the V-blaze on the brow,
the coral-inner ears, the tiny black snout-triangle all land at large size.
The torii pillar mirror is clean and confident, the palette is on-pin, and
the mint-green foxfire is spot-on. But the brief's ENTIRE 32px thesis is
"the WIDE nine-tail peacock fan is the dominant read," and right now it is
NOT. That's the gate, so this is an ITERATE.

## Ranking of aspects

1. STRONGEST — Palette + mint-foxfire separation. Sampled foxfire orb =
   (146,217,191) vs pinned (150,224,196): a near-exact match, and
   unmistakably MINT, with clear daylight between it and Yurei's blue-cyan
   (120,206,232). Coral tips (212,109,68) read warm and distinct. House
   triad (dark-core / flat-fill / top-left sheen) is present and disciplined.
2. STRONG — Face & charm. Scary-cute menace nailed: amber slit-eyes, sly
   brow-blaze, neat ear set. This is the appeal anchor; keep it.
3. STRONG — Torii pillar mirror. Vermilion post + shide-garland + fox-mask
   plaque cap is legible, symmetric on-axis, repeatable. No top-heavy risk.
   The 32px torii strip reads as a torii.
4. WEAKEST — The nine-tail FAN. At 32px the tails collapse into a spiky
   sunburst of short coral-tipped white spikes — it reads "hedgehog" or
   "splat," NOT "peacock fan of nine bushy tails." This is the dominant-read
   requirement and it is failing the gate.
5. WEAK — Body/tail value structure. White fur fan + white haori body + the
   white face are all the same value mass, so the silhouette has no internal
   hierarchy. The fox face does not separate from the tail fan at gameplay
   scale; the 32px read is one undifferentiated white blob with spikes.

## KEEP
- The face construction, ear shape, brow-blaze, and amber eyes — unchanged.
- The exact mint-foxfire hue and the coral/vermilion/gold pins — on-target.
- The torii pillar and its cap. Done.
- The haori body's gold trim + vermilion inner panel — a nice warm focal in
  the chest; it's the only value break that survives shrink. Lean into it.

## FIX (prioritized punch list for round 2)

1. MAKE THE FAN THE SILHOUETTE (top priority). The tails are currently too
   SHORT, too THIN, and too MANY-as-spikes. Redesign as a true peacock fan:
   - Drop the count READ to a clean odd fan — render 9 but group/overlap so
     the EYE reads ~5 broad lobes (center tail tallest, stepping down
     symmetrically). Nine separate thin spikes is noise at 1x.
   - Each tail must be a BUSHY teardrop/leaf lobe at least as wide as it is
     tall near the tip — currently they're pin-thin triangles. Widen each
     lobe ~2-2.5x and round the silhouette so the fan outline is a smooth
     scalloped peacock arc, not a sea-urchin.
   - Spread the fan WIDER than the body is tall so "wide" is the first read.
     The fan should be the dominant mass; the fox face sits in front of it.

2. BUILD VALUE HIERARCHY INTO THE FAN. All-white tails + white body = no
   read. Push the tail fan DARKER/greyer than the face and haori using the
   cool-grey shade (180,176,178) as the tail body, reserving snow-white
   (240,236,232) for the FACE only. That makes the fox face pop forward off
   a slightly receded fan — exactly the "small fox face in front of a wide
   fan" read the brief wants. Coral tips then become the rhythmic accent
   along the fan edge, not the whole silhouette's spike-mass.

3. GROW THE COORDINATED COMB. Right now the coral tips are scattered random
   lengths. Give the fan a clean coral-tip RHYTHM — each lobe capped with a
   matched coral teardrop so the fan edge reads as a deliberate peacock
   pattern (the eye-spots), not frost spikes.

4. 32px SILHOUETTE TEST after the fan rebuild: at actual size the read must
   be "wide fan + small face dot," distinguishable from a hedgehog and from
   any sunburst. If you can't tell it's tails-behind-a-face at 1x, the fan
   isn't wide/bushy enough yet.

5. BODY PROPORTION. The haori body currently reads long and narrow under a
   small fan, inverting the brief (fan should dominate, body is "slim shrine
   body" but SECONDARY). Shorten/compact the body ~15% and lift the fan so
   the fan's width clearly exceeds the body's footprint.

6. MINOR — the large-creature foxfire orb floats well, but at 32px it
   vanishes into the fan spikes. Once the fan is darker (fix 2), the mint
   orb will separate; keep it by ONE ear, drop-shadowed against the fur so
   it survives shrink as the one cool accent.
