# Risón — Round 1 critique (LEAD FACET: FACIAL CHARACTER / EMOTION)

The amber/clay palette, the moustache, the tipped-back sombrero and the warm-bone
skull all land — this is unmistakably a festive calaca and clearly NOT a batch-1
reaper. The anti-reaper guard is satisfied and the prop→pillar mirror is clean.
But the ONE thing this concept exists to do — read as a head-back belly-LAUGH at
32px — does not yet read. The GD's own flag (open-jaw void is interior, so it
doesn't punch the silhouette) is the central unsolved problem, and it sinks the
lead facet. That alone forces ITERATE; the rest is polish.

## Strongest aspect
Palette + anti-reaper identity. Amber (228,168,72) / terracotta clay (196,108,64)
jug, warm-bone skull, rust sombrero band, turquoise held to a true fleck on the
jug glaze (no body-mass turquoise — clean separation from H Jinete). Moustache +
tipped-back sombrero + warm clay = festive Mexican, zero reaper. Lock this.

## Weakest aspect
EMOTION read at gameplay scale. Look at the `silhouette` thumb: it's a lumpy
round skull wearing a hat. There is NO laugh in the black shape — the dropped jaw
is an INTERIOR light/red shape that evaporates the moment the figure is a flat
silhouette against a busy sky. At 32px the open mouth survives only as a faint
mid-value smudge; the "head thrown BACK" tilt barely registers because the
cranium and jaw stack into one near-circular blob. A face-forward concept that
loses its face at gameplay scale is failing its only job.

## Ranked, actionable directives

1. **PUNCH THE JAW INTO THE SILHOUETTE — top priority.** The open mouth must be a
   shape that survives in pure black, not an interior color event. Drop the jaw
   DOWN AND FORWARD so the lower mandible breaks the skull's round contour as a
   distinct jutting wedge — the silhouette outline itself should show a clear
   notch/gap between cranium and dropped jaw. Goal: when you look at the black
   silhouette thumb, you read "open laughing mouth" with zero color. Right now you
   read "ball + hat." This is the whole concept; nothing else matters until it's
   fixed.

2. **Exaggerate the head-BACK tilt in the OUTLINE.** Currently the back-tilt is
   implied by interior shading; the silhouette is nearly vertical. Tilt the whole
   cranium back ~15–20° more so the brow recedes and the chin/jaw thrusts UP-and-
   out — the classic belly-laugh "chin to the sky." The tilt should be legible
   purely from the top-edge contour of the silhouette, not from socket placement.

3. **Sombrero must not eat the skull-back read.** The brim-behind-the-head is
   correct for anti-reaper, but in the 32px thumb the sombrero edge and the
   cranium merge into one mass — losing both the hat tell AND the head-back tilt.
   Add 1–2px of ink-gap / value-step between the back of the skull and the brim so
   the two shapes separate in silhouette. The hat should read as a distinct shape
   stacked behind, not fused.

4. **Sockets: commit to crinkled-with-mirth, not closed-and-asleep.** At 48px the
   eyes read as two flat closed lids — it looks sleepy/peaceful, not roaring with
   laughter. Push the laugh-line crescents: arc the closed sockets UPWARD (happy
   curve, convex-down) and add the corner laugh-tick ink marks the brief calls
   for. A down-curve reads sad/tired; an up-curve reads joy. This is a cheap, high-
   leverage fix for the emotion.

5. **Teeth/tongue: thin the noise.** The lower tooth row at 32px is turning into a
   gray dither band that muddies the mouth. Once the jaw punches the silhouette
   (#1), keep teeth as a SMALL upper row only + one curled tongue-tick of warm
   red — let the negative-space void do the talking. Fewer, bigger tooth blocks
   read; a full dentition turns to mush.

6. **Body is an afterthought — give it ONE clear asymmetry tell.** The brief wants
   one bone hand raising the clay pulque cup (the prop→pillar mirror), the other
   slapping a knee. In the hero the body below the skull is barely present and the
   raised-cup tell is not legible at 32px. The cup is the prop link AND a charm
   beat — make the raised arm + amber cup a clear silhouette appendage so the
   laugh has a reason (he's toasting). Don't over-build the torso; one ribby
   suggestion + the raised cup is enough.

7. **Pillar is good — minor.** The fat clay jug shaft + cup-and-cork cap mirrors
   on-axis and is symmetric; banding reads. Keep it. Only note: warm up the cork/
   cup cap slightly toward amber so the cap doesn't read as a separate cooler
   object at gap-scale. Low priority.
