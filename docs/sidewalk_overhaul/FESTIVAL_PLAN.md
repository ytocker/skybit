## 1. CONCEPT

### The name

**火树夜 — FIRE-TREE NIGHT.** From 火树银花 (*huǒshù yínhuā*, "fire trees, silver flowers"), the thousand-year-old phrase for a lantern-festival night, and a literal description of the show at its centre: 打树花 *dǎshùhuā* / 打铁花 *dǎtiěhuā* — molten iron struck against a wall so it blooms like a tree and falls like a meteor shower. The town's own name for the closing night of the annual parrot fly.

### The identity

The promenade already has a night market. It has one most weekends: stalls, steam, lanterns, people eating standing up. **Tonight is not that.** Tonight is the last night of the tournament, the one night the town's blacksmiths carry the iron pot out to the old wall, the one night the dragon comes out of its cloth, and the one night every stall stays lit past the snow. The market is the town's ordinary self wearing its once-a-year clothes.

This resolves the day plan's Assumption 3 — *"a night market that has a dragon in it every single night isn't a market"* — without breaking it. The dragon is **guaranteed tonight** precisely because tonight is the festival; the rare ≤1-per-run 2% dragon/lion roll stays exactly as specified for **every other phase of the day**. One night a year is not "every night." The day plan's rule survives; the festival earns its exception.

### Why it's the day's climax

Because the day already spent 270 seconds paying for it. The carts arrived at sunset. The frames went up at dusk. Then it **rained on the setup** for eighty seconds and the town built the market anyway, under pitched tarps, in straw capes. The storm is the debt; Fire-Tree Night is the payment. Nothing else in the cycle has that much runway behind it.

### The through-line, and the razor

**Everything at this festival travels upward, or looks upward.**

Steam rises off the steamers. Sparks rise off the wall. Lanterns hang overhead in two interleaved rows. The pearl spins above the pearl-bearer's head. The dragon's snout is *lifted forward into the scroll*. The crowd's glance-up-left rate doubles. Smoke, sparks, steam, lanterns, the pearl, the dragon's head, the crowd's chins — one vector, held for fifty-five seconds.

**The test every beat must pass:** *does it move light upward, or move a face upward?* If a beat does neither, it is dressing, and dressing goes in the gaps between beats, not in the beats. This is also why the festival works in **this** game specifically: the player is the thing above. The town is not performing at a camera. It is performing at Pip.

Two concepts I developed and discarded:

- **"The market that wouldn't close"** — the festival as stubbornness, the town refusing to pack up as the snow comes in. Rejected as a spine: it's a *wind-down* idea, not a climax idea, and it duplicates the storm chapter's emotional move (the town keeps working in bad weather). **Kept as the last two beats** — the tea stall that stays lit into the flakes.
- **"The tournament's closing ceremony"** — banners, a prize, a formal focus. Rejected: it makes the street stare at a stage instead of at the sky, it needs text or an award object to read, and it turns the town into an audience for an event the player can't see. The tournament stays *implied*, through glances only, exactly as §10 of the day plan does it.

## 2. TIMELINE — the run of show

**Window (retimed).** Festival runs **φ 0.680 → 0.820 = t 267.6 s → 322.7 s = 55.1 s**, which at 160 px/s is **8,816 world px ≈ 9.8 blocks**. The original 0.644–0.785 window put a third of the festival inside the rain tail; the new one starts the instant the rain is gone and buys the ~14 s the three-act bill needs. It requires exactly one weather change: snow onset moves φ 0.765 → 0.800 (RETUNE ii). Everything before 267.6 s and after 322.7 s is unchanged.

**Reading the table.** *Frame* is what a 360 px window contains at that moment (360 px = 2.25 s of flight). *Upward* is the beat's compliance with the razor.

### PHASE III — THE CROWN · φ 0.784 – 0.816 · t 308.5 – 321.0 s · 2,000 px

**t 308.5 – 310.5 | φ 0.784 – 0.789 — THE BREATH (2.0 s, one screen-width)**
No stalls. A **lantern arch** spans the promenade at the grandfathered garland height (`GROUND_Y − 118`). Beneath it, near-empty wet paving. Along both decks, figures are standing **still, turned right, upstream** — waiting for something the player cannot see yet. Density reads high but motion reads zero.
The drum from beat 1 is back, close now: one thud every 1.05 s.
*Frame:* an arch, an empty lit street, and forty motionless people facing the way you're flying.
*Story:* the only two seconds of held tension in the cycle. It exists so the dragon arrives *into* silence.

**t 310.5 – 321.0 | φ 0.789 – 0.816 — ★★ THE DRAGON**
See §4. **10.5 seconds**, because the procession travels *with* the scroll at 0.55×, so the player flies alongside it rather than past it. Reveal order as it enters from the right edge: **pearl → head → seven carriers → tail → drum-and-cymbal cart.** The full 390 px set never fits in a 360 px frame, deliberately — the player never sees the whole dragon at once, so it reads as longer than it is.

**The market pauses.** Across the two blocks the dragon occupies, every stall drops to non-working: the vendor steps out from behind the counter to the front edge, arms at sides, facing right; calls stop; steam thins from three wisps to one. The market's own signature — steam and noise — is withdrawn for ten seconds and given back when the tail passes. *This is the single most important beat in the plan.* Nothing says "this is the big one" like the market shutting up to watch it.

**t 314.8 | φ 0.800 — the first snowflake**, 4.3 s into the dragon. Not a coincidence and not a coincidence the player will consciously notice: the crown of the night gets snow falling through it, lantern halos drawn **over** the flake layer so the snow is lit. The dragon dances for another six seconds anyway.
*Sound feel:* drum and cymbal, no crowd noise underneath — the crowd is quiet because it's looking.
*Upward:* the pearl spins overhead in a figure-8; the head is snout-lifted; the crowd's chins are up; and at t ≈ 317 s **the dragon's head turns up-left toward Pip for 1.4 s.** The town's tournament awareness, cashed in once, at the highest possible moment, with no text and no sound sting.

### ★★ THE DRAGON — the crown · once, guaranteed, φ 0.789

The existing `perf_dragon_dance` is intact and is the centrepiece. What's added is **staging**, not art surgery.

**The procession, in reveal order (right edge → left):**

| Element | Width | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| **Pearl-bearer** — one figure, pole held overhead, a 10 px amber sphere tracing a figure-8 at 0.8 Hz, capped halo r8 | 40 px | 🆕 NEW ART |
| gap | 30 px | |
| **Dragon** — head, 7 carriers, 156 px body, tail; flanking crowd | 230 px | existing, unchanged |
| gap | 20 px | |
| **Drum-and-cymbal cart** — the existing seated drummer at 1.5× on a two-wheel chassis, flanked by 2 cymbal figures | 70 px | 🆕 NEW ART (reuses `draw_cart_folded` wheels + existing drummer) |
| **Total** | **390 px** | |

**Why the pearl leads and the drum follows.** Convention has the pearl-chaser ahead of the head (a sphere representing wisdom, which the dragon chases) and percussion — one to two drummers plus cymbals and gongs — accompanying. Physically the drum often rides at the front; here it rides at the back, because the player's reveal order runs right-to-left and the dramatically correct sequence is *mystery → face → mass → drum*. Tai Hang's narrow-route practice, where percussion trails the head down a street too narrow to lead it, is the precedent that makes this honest.

**Parade drift — the mechanic that makes it the crown.** The set piece translates at **+0.55 × scroll (88 px/s in Pip's direction)** for its whole lifetime. Relative closing speed 72 px/s; the 390 px procession takes **(360 + 390) / 72 ≈ 10.4 s** to cross the frame, against 3.7 s for a static prop. The player does not fly *past* the dragon, the player flies *with* it. It is the only object in the game that keeps pace.

**Staging:**
- **Route:** two blocks (6,300 – 8,100 px), zero stalls placed, both decks lined with spectators at 2.4 px spacing, all facing right (upstream) until the head passes them, then turning to follow.
- **Parade-follow crowd:** 6–9 figures walk *behind the tail at the dragon's speed*, the only far-lane figures all night moving at non-scroll velocity.
- **Market pause:** the two stalls at the route's edges stop working (vendor out front, steam 3 wisps → 1, calls off) for the traverse + 1.5 s, then restart staggered.
- **The glance:** at t ≈ 317 s the dragon's head turns up-left for 1.4 s. Once. Never repeated.
- **Snow:** first flakes at 314.8 s fall through it, halos drawn over the flake layer.

**Cadence:** exactly **one per cycle**, deterministic at φ 0.789. Outside the festival window the existing rare-roll (≤1 per run, ~2% per eligible block, phase ≥ 0.60) is unchanged and can still land a lion dance on an ordinary night.

**Update — both acts now guaranteed, at different points (post-launch revision).** The original plan suppressed the lion tonight on the theory that two marquee acts in one night halves both. Player feedback overruled that: the dragon and the lion are the two things being asked for, so both are guaranteed every festival, staged as separate sightings rather than rivals for the same slot — the lion is not a second crown, it's an earlier, smaller sighting that makes the dragon's later arrival feel like an escalation rather than a repeat. See the new §4a below for its placement.

**Gate change required:** the existing `storm_intensity == 0` condition must become `storm_intensity < 0.25`, or the first flakes cancel the crown.

### ★ THE LION — the earlier sighting · once, guaranteed, φ 0.762

The existing `perf_lion_dance` is intact and unchanged: two dancers under one flowing costume (a carried bulbous head trailing a segmented cloth body), its own drummer beside it, its own flanking crowd — a shorter, self-contained troupe with no staged procession to build (no pearl, no cart; the pearl is the dragon's to chase).

**Where it sits, and why there.** `foreground_weekend.happening()` latches a beat's start the first frame `phase` reaches `window[0]`, so every beat's start second is deterministic, not randomized inside its window — which makes the festival's real, un-narrated gaps computable exactly. The Monkey King's troupe (§4, ★ Show Square 2) ends at t = 299.7 s; the dragon's own held "breath" wants silence from ≈ 308.5 s. That leaves an ~8.8 s stretch that nothing else claims. The lion's 6 s act starts right after the troupe, at φ 0.762 (t ≈ 299.8 s), and finishes by t ≈ 305.8 s — a clean ≈2.7 s pause still lands before the breath, so nothing collides.

**Cadence:** exactly one per cycle, deterministic at φ 0.762 — same guarantee as the dragon, same "once, not a roll" mechanism.

**Staging:** simple screen-crossing, right to left, over its 6 s (`sx = (W+90) − k·(W+90+150)`) — the same interpolation the dragon uses for its own procession, just without the extra cart/pearl set pieces since the act carries none.

**Why it doesn't pause the market.** The market-pause beat (`parade_active()`) stays keyed to the dragon alone. Pausing for the lion too would spend the plan's single "the market stops for this" gesture twice in one night and dilute the dragon's claim to being the crown — the lion parades past a market that's still working, which is exactly what keeps it reading as the smaller of the two sightings.

### ★ THE THIRD ACT — THE MONKEY KING'S TROUPE · once, φ 0.756

**The monkey question, resolved.** No live-animal act. Traditional 耍猴 street monkey performance is in steep decline (Xinye County's trainers fell from ~10,000 to ~300), is legally unprotected, and is actively contested on welfare grounds — a costumed macaque on a leash would read to a modern casual-game audience as *sad*, not *charming*, and would be the one beat in the day the player felt bad about. It also fails the razor: a monkey on a chain looks **down**.

**What earns the slot instead.** **Three masked acrobats as Sun Wukong** — the Monkey King. Human performers, a temple-fair staple alongside stilt-walkers, yangge and lion dance, and a direct sibling of the existing bian-lian mask-changer act (A8), so the family already exists in the codebase's idiom.

- **The mask** (the whole read at 22 px): gold face, red-brown fur ruff, and two long swept phoenix plumes off the brow. Unmistakable in silhouette from every other head in the game.
- **Beat 1 — the staff spin** (2.0 s): a 20 px bar rotating around one figure at 3 Hz, 4-frame cycle. The most legible acrobatic motion available at this pixel scale.
- **Beat 2 — the shoulder tower** (2.4 s): two-high, a 34 px silhouette, the tallest human shape in the festival, locked on a gong hit.
- **Beat 3 — the somersault** (1.5 s): the third figure rolls 30 px across the front of the ring as a tumbling ball.
- Ring of 5 spectators, near deck, desynced clapping.

**The payoff.** In the block immediately after, **~1 kid in 3 wears an 8-px paper monkey mask**. They bought it from the merchandise kiosk. It's the only visible cause-and-effect on the street all day, it makes the troupe feel like it *happened* rather than *played*, and it costs one accessory sprite.

### Rejected: the sky-lantern release

Tempting, thematically perfect, and **cut**. Sky lanterns would rise into the pillar corridor, compete with the coin for the player's eye, and risk reading as an obstacle or a pickup. The festival's upward vector is expressed by steam, sparks, the pearl and the crowd's chins — none of which enter the play space.

## 5. CROWD

**The tell that separates the festival from the rest of the day is *facing*.** The morning market crowd (Ch2) faces **away** from stalls, carrying baskets — morning markets are errands. The festival crowd faces **in and up** — night markets are meals and shows. Same 57-pedestrian pool, opposite body language, and the difference reads at 18 px.

**The seven festival behaviours** (all latched at slot entry, all with per-instance phase offsets):

| **Walk-and-eat** | `reach_up` retargeted to chest height holding a **tanghulu** (3 red beads on a stick), a **sugar-painting disc**, a paper cone or a skewer; gait −15% | Everywhere. **The dominant festival behaviour** — night-market seating is sparse, so supper is a grazing stroll, not a seated effort |

**Animals.** The 9-dog and 7-critter pools run at festival weight: a dog under every third stall, and the day plan's **dropped-noodles** once-per-day beat is retained, rescheduled to Strip B (t ≈ 294 s) where there's a stall to drop them at.

## 7. NARRATIVE ARC

**Shape: a two-crest market, a held breath, and a late crown that walks beside you — then snow.**

Fire-Tree Night opens on an empty, perfect, still-wet street, which is the only clean look at the set anyone gets. Four seconds later the town floods back in and the market runs its two crests: first the raw density crest at 285 s (six lit structures, seven steam plumes, twenty-six people, and the coin still winning by eighty luma), then — after a fire show and a deliberately ordinary recovery block — the second crest at 305 s, which is *smaller in crowd and larger in incident*, because that's how the second half of a good night goes. The shows are spaced so that no two peaks touch: crest, fire, ordinary, troupe, crest, and then two full seconds of a lantern arch over near-empty paving with forty people standing dead still, facing the way you're flying.

Then the dragon, and the dragon is the crown for a mechanical reason as much as a dramatic one: it **travels with the scroll**, so instead of three and a half seconds of a set piece sliding past, the player gets ten and a half seconds of flying alongside a parade — and the market **stops working** to watch it, withdrawing its own signature of steam and noise for the duration. Four seconds in, the first snowflake falls through it. Six seconds after that the dragon's head turns up-left at Pip for one and a bit seconds, and that is the entire tournament, cashed in once, with no text.

Everything early is planted for something late. The covered lump on the handcart at 170 s is the dragon's head. The dark scaffold standing in the rain at 250 s is the iron flower. The storm's lightning that flattened the street into a silhouette is answered by the burst that rims it in warm light. The rain that soaked the setup is the reason the lanterns double in the stone, and the water the fire crew pours on the paving is the reason the sparks do too. The paper mask a kid wears at 305 s was bought at 300 s. And the market that stayed open under a pitched tarp at storm peak is the same market that stays lit into the first flakes — which is exactly how the festival hands the street back to the small hours, on time, at φ 0.820, with a smoking scaffold, a scorch field, and seven people carrying a dragon home.

## 8. SOURCES & INSPIRATION

- **打铁花 / 打树花 datiehua–dashuhua (molten-iron fireworks)** — Northern-Song origin, blacksmiths who couldn't afford fireworks striking 1,600 °C scrap iron against a wall so it blooms like a chrysanthemum and falls like a meteor shower; performers work in soaked straw hats and sheepskin. This is the single most load-bearing piece of research in the plan: it is the one fire form whose signature is **spark count and arc**, not brightness, which is the only kind of fire that survives a 150-luma night cap. It gave the festival its name (火树银花), its apparatus, its crew silhouette, its four-burst cadence, and the doused-paving apron. ([Olachina — Datiehua](https://olachina.org/datiehua/), [Dashuhua — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashuhua), [RADII — Ringing in the New Year with Iron Flowers](https://radii.co/article/ringing-in-the-new-year-with-iron-flowers), [Amusing Planet — Da Shuhua](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2024/02/da-shuhua-fireworks-of-molten-iron.html))
- **Dragon-dance staging** — a standard set is one dragon, one pearl-ball, drum and cymbal; the dance is led by a bearer controlling the pearl (wisdom) that the head chases; percussion runs one to two drummers plus three to four cymbal/gong players. Directly produced the pearl-bearer, the drum-and-cymbal cart, and the procession's element order. ([Vancouver Lion Dance — Dragon vs Lion](https://www.vancouverliondance.com/post/dragon-dance-vs-lion-dance), [Dragon Vibe — Dragon Dance in China](https://dragon-vibe.com/blogs/dragon-blog/dragon-dance-in-china), [Rappler — Join a Chinese dragon dance](https://www.rappler.com/life-and-style/49348-dare-rappler-chinese-dragon-dance/))
- **Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance** — a 67 m incense-stick dragon led by **two pomelo "dragon pearls" spun for twirling radiance**, paraded through narrow streets with percussion, trailing smoke and sparks "like fairy dust." Confirmed the pearl-in-front staging, justified percussion trailing the head on a narrow route, and is the precedent for smoke + sparks + dragon reading as one coherent tradition rather than two bolted-together shows. ([HKTB — Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance](https://www.discoverhongkong.com/eng/events/tai-hang-s-fire-dragon-dance.html), [SCMP — a raucous tradition since the Qing dynasty](https://www.scmp.com/postmag/culture/article/3326977/tai-hangs-legendary-fire-dragon-about-return-all-its-glory), [National Geographic — Watch Hong Kong's Dazzling Fire Dragon Dance](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/tai-hang-fire-dragon-dance))
- **Taiwan night-market layout and eating culture** — Raohe Street is "one long street with food stalls on both sides **and a row running down the middle**"; Shilin is interconnected alleys; seating is sparse, so supper is "a grazing stroll rather than a single seated effort"; the staple is *xiaochi*, small eats; **tanghulu** is fruit in a crackling hot-sugar shell. This produced the market-strip pitch, the mid-deck spine row, walk-and-eat as the dominant crowd behaviour, and the tanghulu skewer as the most legible 18-px food prop available. ([Michelin Guide — Navigating Taipei's Night Markets](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/travel/best-night-market-taipei-food-travel-guide), [Taiwanderers — Best Taiwan Night Markets](https://taiwanderers.com/best-taiwan-night-markets/), [Stroll for Your Supper](https://deiwei.substack.com/p/stroll-for-your-supper), [ThinkChinatown — Night Market](https://www.thinkchinatown.org/night-market-copy))
- **Temple-fair (庙会) repertoire** — dragon and lion dances, **stilt walking**, yangge, waist-drum dancing, shadow puppetry, plus interactive stands for **sugar painting**, lantern riddles, calligraphy, and craft stalls selling **hand-painted masks** and clay figurines; stilt walking is specifically the northern signature. Produced the travelling stilt-walkers, the sugar-painter theatre stall, and the paper-mask souvenir payoff. ([CITS — Temple Fair, Miaohui](https://www.cits.net/china-travel-guide/temple-fair-a-traditional-cultural-gathering-in-china.html), [Beijing Municipal Government — Temple Fairs](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/beijinginfo/culture/beijingfolklore/templefairs/202110/t20211018_2514734.html), [china.org.cn — A General Introduction to Miaohui](http://www.china.org.cn/english/travel/85304.htm))
- **耍猴 street monkey performance** — trainer numbers in Xinye County collapsed from ~10,000 to ~300; performances are contested on welfare grounds ("inhumane and harmful to animals"); China has no general anti-cruelty statute. This is why the monkey slot went to **masked human acrobats as Sun Wukong** rather than a live-animal act: the modern read on a chained macaque is sad, not charming, and it fails the festival's upward razor. ([China.org.cn — Traditional monkey circus show faces dilemma](http://www.china.org.cn/arts/2014-10/30/content_33915665_2.htm), [The World of Chinese — The show must not go on](https://www.theworldofchinese.com/2022/08/the-show-must-not-go-on/), [Animal welfare and rights in China — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_welfare_and_rights_in_China))
- **Qinhuai Lantern Fair** — lantern-festival street fairs as a distinct dressing register (arches, dense overhead lantern lattices) rather than a stall register; the source for the lantern arch at the breath and over the dragon route. ([Qinhuai Lantern Fair — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinhuai_Lantern_Fair))
