# BIG REAPY — round 1 critique (art-director)

VERDICT: ITERATE

A genuinely strong first take. The house-style correction landed: this is FLAT,
SATURATED, warm-bone-bold chibi — zero drift to the prior round's grey-realist
skull. The giant-head-on-tiny-cloak silhouette is unmistakable and distinct from
the other four. It is not ship-ready yet for two reasons that compound at 1×: the
grin currently reads more "buck-toothed / slightly grim" than "delighted puppy,"
and the bident prop fails the prop->pillar test — the GD's self-flag on the
prongs is correct and the pillar pair (cell b) is the weakest thing on the sheet.

## Strongest / weakest

- STRONGEST: the showcase silhouette (cell a). The 55% skull on the ash-blue
  poncho is bold, original, and the value structure (warm bone vs ash-blue cloak
  vs ink keyline) holds beautifully in BOTH the day/night insets AND grayscale —
  the accessibility guardrail is already met: the dark socket + keyline carry the
  face without the ember. That is the hardest thing to get right and it's done.
- WEAKEST: the PROP->PILLAR mirror (cell b). At pillar scale the bone shaft reads
  as a pale near-featureless post — the vertebra bumps mostly vanish — and the
  fork caps read as a thin frail wishbone, not a menacing soul-catcher. This is
  the gate this concept has to clear (every pick must mirror into a clean tileable
  pillar pair) and right now it's the thing most likely to sink the pick.

## KEEP

- The warm-bone-dominant + ash-blue + single-ember-accent palette. Bold, on-brief,
  classic-skull family, disjoint from the other four. Don't touch the hues.
- The ash-blue cloak as the value anchor — it's exactly what stops the warm bone
  flattening on the warm day sky. Confirmed working in the day inset.
- Big round deep-set sockets with the ember pinprick + the lifted (bowed-up) bone
  brows. This is the right "excited" lever; keep the brows lifted, never a V.
- Grayscale legibility (cell c). Ship-grade. The socket-shape read survives.
- The tiny peeking feet + brass collar clasp — clean value break, cute, on-style.

## FIX (ranked)

1. THE GRIN reads grim, not delighted. Two problems: (a) only two big square front
   teeth dominate with stubby flankers — at 1× this is a "buck-toothed underbite,"
   not a happy jack-grin; (b) the upward bow is too subtle — the tooth band reads
   nearly flat, and a flat mouth on a skull defaults to grim. The whole "scary-cute
   not horror" mandate lives here and it's the round's biggest miss.
2. THE BIDENT FORK is short, frail, and ambiguous (GD flag CONFIRMED). The prongs
   sweep out then hook only slightly; at showcase they read as a thin wishbone and
   at pillar scale they nearly disappear. A two-prong "soul-catcher" should read as
   a bold, chunky, clearly-pronged fork even at 1×. Right now the prop's identity
   (what separates it from Grim Sprout's scythe and a plain bone post) is too weak.
3. SOUL-WISP glow placement is weak (GD flag CONFIRMED). The ember teardrop sits
   low between the prongs and the additive halo is so tight it barely separates
   from the warm bone — it reads as a smudge, not a captured soul. It needs to sit
   clearly in the fork's cradle (the gap between the tips) with a tighter, brighter
   hot core so it reads as a discrete caught object, not bone bleed.
4. THE PILLAR SHAFT loses its banding at scale. The vertebra knuckles + groove
   rings smoothscale away to a near-blank pale post in cell b. A bone-spine pillar
   has to read as segmented vertebrae even when scrolling — otherwise it's an
   anonymous tan bar and the "prop mirrors into THIS pillar" story collapses.
5. NIGHT SOCKET RISK. In the night inset the ember + ink sockets sit in a bone
   face on a dark sky — fine — but the ember halo is doing almost nothing. Push the
   socket ember a touch on night only so the eyes read as lit, not as two dark
   holes (don't overdo it — the bone+keyline is already carrying the silhouette).
6. JAW/CHEEK polish. The cheekbone hollows are a hair muddy where they meet the
   jaw trapezoid — at showcase there's a faint double-seam on the lower-left. Minor,
   but tidy the jaw inset so the chin reads as one clean bone shape.

## Iteration directives (next-round punch list)

1. Re-cut the grin: go to FOUR-to-FIVE evenly-sized square teeth (not 2 big + 2
   stub), raise the upward bow ~40-50% so the tooth band visibly SMILES, and widen
   the dark mouth-seat so the gaps between teeth read as a clean curved smile-band
   at 1×. Target read: a happy pumpkin jack-grin, not an underbite.
2. Beef the bident fork: ~1.5-2x the prong thickness and length, hook the tips in
   harder so the two prongs clearly CRADLE a gap (a readable U/V soul-catcher), and
   keep them bold enough to survive the 1x pillar downscale. Make the fork the
   prop's signature — it must not read as "generic bone stick."
3. Reseat the soul-wisp: move the ember teardrop UP into the cradle between the
   prong tips, shrink the soft halo radius but raise its alpha, and add a tight
   EMBER_HOT core so it reads as one discrete glowing caught soul, not a bone smear.
4. Make the vertebra banding survive downscale: fewer, CHUNKIER, higher-contrast
   knuckle segments with a darker groove ring between each, spaced so 2-3 read
   across a gameplay-height pillar. Test it in cell b at the actual obstacle size,
   not just showcase.
5. Night-only: nudge the socket ember glow up so the eyes read lit on the dark sky;
   leave day/grayscale as-is (they already pass).
6. Tidy the jaw inset seam on the lower-left cheek so the chin is one clean shape.

## Distinctness / feasibility — both fine

- Distinct from Grim Sprout (tiny imp+scythe), Dr. Quill (beak), Tick-Tock
  (hourglass clerk), The Hollow (faceless void): giant-skull body block + bone-fork
  prop + warm-bone palette collide with none of them. No change needed.
- Fully procedural; no sprite-sheet thinking. The fixes above are all geometry/
  value tweaks, not new techniques.

Re-evaluate next round primarily on (1) does the grin read delighted at 1×, and
(2) does cell b's pillar pair read as a bold pronged bone-spine — those two gate
the pick.
